Why this

I like sentences that have to be right about themselves, and I wanted to see whether one could also get its own history right. You said it's about the journey, so the journey went into the sentence.

A sentence that counts its own letters is called an autogram, and you can't just sit down and write one. Change a seven to an eight and the sentence loses an s, an e, a v and an n, and gains an i, a g, an h and a t. Now other claims are false, and fixing those breaks more.

So a small program guessed. It began with "one a, one b, one c" and on every step corrected about a quarter of the claims at random. Each version also states exactly how many false versions came before it, so that number is always right and only the letters can be wrong. The walk ended the first time they all came out true.

How close it got

A typical lie got 11 or 12 of its 26 claims wrong. Across the whole walk, 259 lies had two claims wrong and 15 had just one. Only the final sentence had none.

The near misses led nowhere. The last one came almost 9 million lies before the end, and one step before the truth, 9 claims were still false. Then all of them were right at once. I didn't expect that, and it's my favorite part.

Walk it again

The number of lies is a claim too, so you can check it. This runs the same walk from the same random seed in your browser, using one CPU core. It took about 20 seconds on the mini.

Make your own

Write how your sentence begins, and your browser will look for a version that's true. The walk adds "told exactly … lies before it could truthfully say that it has" and all 26 counts. On the mini, nine walks in ten would finish in about 90 seconds or less.

The small print

  • I chose the random seed, today's date (20261105), before the one run of this sentence, and kept what came out. Earlier test runs used different wording, so none of their tries count here.
  • Four separate counts agree. The C program found it, a Python script recounted the final letters, a second program wrote out all 88,426,058 sentences in full and counted each one from its text, and the JavaScript on this page stops at the same place.
  • It took 3.6 seconds on one core at the lowest priority. I'd expected billions of tries and a long wait. Lee Sallows, who coined the word autogram, built dedicated hardware to hunt for them in the 1980s.
  • I also ran 160 walks from other seeds, so other true sentences exist with other numbers. The median walk took about 104 million lies, the shortest 1,360,479 and the longest about 2.7 billion. This one ran longer than 41% of them.
  • Assuming a generous 10 watts, your 20.19¢/kWh puts the electricity for that run at about 0.0002¢.
  • The code is on the mini in ~/Development/honest-eventually.
